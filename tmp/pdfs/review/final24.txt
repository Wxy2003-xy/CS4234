(b) Prove or disprove the following statement: Given a flow network G, if we replace a
directed edge e with two directed edges e1 and e2 in opposite directions, and with the
same capacity, then the value of the maximum flow remains unchanged.
Solution: The statement is false.
Consider a simple example where the graph G contains a single directed edge from t
to s with capacity 10. In this case, the maximum flow in G is 0, as there is no path
from s to t.
If we replace the directed edge e with two directed edges e1 (from t to s) and e2 (from
s to t) with the same capacity, the residual capacity allows a flow of 10 through the
edge e2. Consequently, the maximum flow increases to 10.
Hence, the value of the maximum flow does not remain unchanged.

we wish to pack the pieces into bins, where each bin can hold any subset of pieces of total
size at most 1. Our goal is to find a packing that minimizes the number of bins used.
Find a polynomial-time algorithm that gives us a packing which uses OU T bins, such that
OU T ≤ 2OP T + 1, where OP T is the minimum number of bins needed to pack the n items.
Solution: The algorithm is straightforward: when processing ai, check if ai can be placed
in any of the currently open bins. If it cannot, open a new bin and place ai in the newly
opened bin.
We claim that at most one bin can remain strictly less than half-full at any point. To see
why, suppose there were two bins that were less than half-full. In this case, the items in the
second bin (the later-opened bin) could have been placed in the first bin (the
earlier-opened bin), contradicting our assumption.
Now, let t be the number of bins that are more than half-full. The total size of items in
these bins is at least t/2, as each bin contributes at least half its capacity. Thus,P ai ≥ t/2.
Since the total size of the items P ai must fit into OP T bins, we have P ai ≤ OP T.
Therefore, t ≤ 2OP T.
Additionally, at most one bin can remain less than half-full, so the total number of bins
used is OU T ≤ t + 1 ≤ 2OP T + 1, as required.

Assume you are given a connected, undirected graphG = (V, E) where every node has degree
2k for some integer k ≥ 1. An orientation of G assigns a direction to every edge in E, i.e.,
for each edge ( u, v), it outputs either ( u → v) or ( v → u). The result is a directed graph
orient(G).
Prove that there exists an orientation of the graph so that the directed graph orient( G) is
strongly connected. (Recall that in a strongly connected graph, there is a directed path
between every pair of nodes in both directions.)

Solution: Since the graph G is connected and every node has even degree, there exists an
Eulerian cycle in G that visits every edge exactly once. We use this Eulerian cycle to
construct the orientation.
Orient the edges according to the Eulerian cycle as follows: assume the Eulerian cycle is
given by the sequence of edges e1, e2, e3, . . . , em. For each edge ei = (u, v) in the cycle, if
the next edge in the cycle is ei+1 = (v, w), orient ei as (u → v). This ensures that each edge
is oriented in the direction of traversal along the cycle.
Since the Eulerian cycle visits every node in the graph, it guarantees that every pair of
nodes is connected by a directed path following the cycle (in the correct direction).
Therefore, the graph orient( G) is strongly connected.
If the Eulerian cycle forms the entire graph, we are done. Otherwise, remove the edges of
the Eulerian cycle from G and apply induction on the remaining edges. By induction, the
remaining subgraph can also be oriented to form a strongly connected directed graph.
Combining the orientations of the Eulerian cycle and the remaining subgraph ensures that
the entire graph orient( G) is strongly connected.
Thus, there exists an orientation of G such that orient(G) is strongly connected.
