===== PDF PAGE 10 =====
Expectation is a weighted average of outcomes
10
For a discrete random variable X:
E[X] = ∑ₓ x · Pr[X = x]
For a fair die: E[X] = (1+2+3+4+5+6)/6 = 3.5
Expectation summarizes a distribution—weighted average ofattainable outcomes.

===== PDF PAGE 14 =====

14
For e = (u,v), the endpoints receive independent fair bits.

Analyze one edge; linearity does the rest.

===== PDF PAGE 15 =====
Random cut is a 2-approximation
15

OPT is at most the total edge weight, while the random cut gets half of that total weight in expectation.
A simple local random choice yields a global factor-1/2 guarantee.

===== PDF PAGE 16 =====
Max-SAT asks us to satisfy as many clauses of a CNFas possible
16
Literal A Boolean variable xⱼ or its negation ¬xⱼ.
Clause An OR of literals, e.g. Cᵢ = x₁ ∨ ¬x₂ ∨ x₄.
CNF An AND of clauses: Φ = C₁ ∧ C₂ ∧ ··· ∧ Cₘ.
Max-SAT Choose an assignment maximizing the satisfied clause weight.
SAT asks whether all clauses can be satisfied; Max-SAT optimizes how many.

===== PDF PAGE 17 =====
A concrete formula separates satisfying assignments
17
Formula
Φ = (¬x₂ ∨ x₃) ∧ (x₁ ∨ ¬x₂ ∨ x₃) ∧ (¬x₁ ∨ x₂ ∨ x₄)
Two assignments
Satisfying:  x₁=T, x₂=F, x₃=F, x₄=T
Not satisfying:  x₁=T, x₂=F, x₃=F, x₄=F
The objective counts the clauses made true by the assignment.

A two-literal clause is satisfied with probability 3/4
19
The clause fails only when both literals are false.
Pr[clause satisfied] = 1 − (½)² = ¾
So E[# satisfied] = ¾m ≥ ¾ · OPT because OPT ≤ m.
Random assignment is a 4/3-approximation for Max-E2SAT.

===== PDF PAGE 38 =====
Conditional expectation turns choices into decisions
38
Suppose the current partial assignment has conditional expectation W and p = Pr[xⱼ=true]
W = p·E[cost | xⱼ=true] + (1−p)·E[cost | xⱼ=false]
At least one branch has conditional expectation at least W; choose it deterministically.
A weighted average cannot exceed both of its components.
